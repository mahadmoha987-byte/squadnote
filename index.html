Teammate feedback

Solo queue puts you into a match with four people you may never have played with. Squadnote is a Windows app built on Overwolf that shows how previous teammates described playing with them.

The planned overlay places a short community feedback summary beneath each teammate during Competitive agent select. It covers communication, teamwork, composure and playstyle—including strengths and habits other players found frustrating.

HOW IT WORKS

From a finished match
to a useful summary.

Feedback comes from people who played together. The next teammate sees the recurring pattern, not the individual review.

  1. 01

    Link your account with Riot Sign On

    Sign in through Riot to verify your account. Squadnote does not ask for your Riot password. Squadnote will follow Riot’s guidance on player opt-in and data sharing.

  2. 02

    Choose tags after a Competitive match

    A short post-game popup lets you tag the teammates you just played with. Tags cover communication, teamwork, composure and playstyle. Shared feedback uses tags only: no free-text reviews, comments or public notes. The planned rule is one feedback submission per match per teammate, after verifying that you shared the match.

  3. 03

    Wait for multiple reports

    A single review does not create a public summary. Multiple different players must report a recurring pattern before it can appear. Feedback stays unavailable until a minimum review threshold is met. The threshold will be published before launch. If there is not enough feedback, the overlay says so instead of filling the gap with a guess.

  4. 04

    Read a short summary in agent select

    In Competitive agent select, the overlay shows aggregated, qualified tendencies such as “players often say…”. Summaries describe tendencies reported by the community, both strengths and frustrating habits, worded as reported patterns and never as verdicts. Individual reviews are never shown, there is no opponent scouting, and the overlay does not provide live tactical advice.

WHAT YOU SEE

How they play
with other people.

A summary of other teammates’ experiences, read before the match begins.

Communication, teamwork and composure

A scoreboard tells you about a player’s performance in a match. It tells you less about whether they communicate clearly, work with their teammates or stay composed when the score turns against them. Those are the experiences Squadnote’s tags are meant to capture.

Strengths and frustrating habits

The same person can be helpful on comms and get frustrated when losing. A useful summary should be able to reflect both. Wording such as “players often say…” makes the source clear: this is community feedback, not a verified finding or a prediction of how the next match will go.

The summary is shared. The review is not.

Other Squadnote users would see the combined summary during Competitive agent select. They would not see individual submissions or who left feedback about whom. Optional private notes are a separate planned feature: free text visible only to the person who wrote it, never an input to the shared summary.

Separate from Riot’s reporting system

Squadnote does not issue penalties, change matchmaking or send community tags as official reports to Riot. It does not assign a skill rank or tell you whom to dodge. For conduct that needs an official report, use VALORANT’s reporting tools.

FEEDBACK RULES

A pattern needs
more than one voice.

These are proposed safeguards for the shared-feedback system, not features already operating in the prototype.

Start with an actual shared match

The planned system verifies that the reviewer and teammate were in the same match. It is not an open profile where anyone can leave a review of someone they have never played with.

Count different players, not repeated submissions

The minimum review threshold is intended to require feedback from multiple different players. Playing several matches with the same reviewer should not make one person’s opinion look like a community consensus.

Check for coordinated or unreliable feedback

The design calls for checks on premade-party feedback, new reviewer accounts and sudden bursts of submissions about one player. Those checks and their limits still need to be built and tested. Tags and thresholds alone do not make feedback accurate or prevent abuse.

Resolve the sharing rules before launch

Squadnote will follow Riot’s guidance on player opt-in and data sharing. Who can have a visible summary, and whether summaries may include frustrating habits, remain subject to that guidance. This page is a product proposal, not a claim of Riot or Overwolf approval.
